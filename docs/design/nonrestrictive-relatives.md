# Nonrestrictive relatives: first implementation from the literature map

Implemented 6–7 October 2026 in the native English Classical and Constructive
grammars. This is a bounded construction family, not a claim of complete
relative-clause coverage. It needs no model call.

## Analysis and provenance

Cann, Kempson and Marten, *The Dynamics of Language*, §3.1.1–3.1.2,
printed pp.74–80, gives the analysis: LINK from an identified entity into a
new proposition; copy that entity onto an unfixed node; MERGE at its argument
position; return to the host; conjoin matrix and supplemental propositions.
The inspected source is the final draft dated 12 December 2004, PDF pp.84–90.
Particularly relevant are LINK adjunction (3.6), the relativizer (3.9), and
nonrestrictive LINK evaluation (3.13). Howes and Gibson (2021), §2.5.1,
p.272, gives a later worked illustration of the same mechanisms.

The source's illustrative sentence is “John, who Sue likes, smokes.” The
workbench uses analogous sentences from its existing vocabulary rather than
adding special entries just to reproduce that example. Test sentences are
authored engineering probes, not independently collected judgments.

For `John, who Mary knows, walks.` both native modes derive:

```
walk(john) ∧ know(mary, john)
```

This differs from restrictive `a man who Mary knows walks`: there the relative
refines the noun before the determiner applies. The nonrestrictive does not
change `john` into a new witness, or modify the meaning of the name.

The Classical implementation follows the cited conjunctive analysis. The
Constructive implementation is our adaptation using native typed conjunction
and local witness closure. Neither the 2005 analysis nor the 2025 Constructive
paper is represented as already supplying this exact executable adaptation.
Coq checks well-formed exported propositions under their declared assumptions;
it does not prove the empirical correctness of this linguistic account.

## Derivation and code

`nonrestrictive_effects.py` executes ordinary recorded operations through
`_sequence`: `make`, `go`, `put`, and `delete`. The existing `merge-relative`
rule performs MERGE and retains the gap marker at its fixed destination.

1. An opening comma at a completed matrix subject or direct object creates
   its `L` daughter with a propositional requirement. The host waits for return;
   the matrix root waits for LINK evaluation.
2. `who` or `which` copies the host onto `L*`. It introduces subject and
   predicate requirements in the new clause.
3. MERGE fixes the copy at `L0` or `L10`. A missing argument, extra object,
   or unresolved unfixed position prevents completion.
4. A closing comma requires the entire relative to be complete. A full stop
   can close a relative on a matrix object at the end of the sentence.
   Local indefinite witnesses close inside the supplemental proposition.
5. After the matrix has composed, optional completion-time LINK evaluation
   conjoins the two propositions. The separate LINKed tree remains visible.

The action trace therefore shows the source analysis's essential steps;
the Python effect does not accept a model-written formula or fabricate a
completed tree. Grammar entries are generated by
`scripts/build_nonrestrictive_extensions.py`, also called by the everyday
grammar builder.

Modifier propagation now stops at a relative's LINK root. It must not erase
the host name while recomputing an adverb inside the relative. After final
conjunction, the matrix is marked against overwriting by an earlier VP.
Modifiers can attach before that final evaluation; DS backtracking keeps
that order available.

## Covered and deferred

Covered: main-clause named subject/direct-object hosts; subject/direct-object
relative gaps; `who`/`which`; local negation; supported adverbs on either
proposition; indefinites inside the relative; supplements on both matrix
arguments. Hosts must already denote an identified constant, without pending
witnesses. `who` versus `which` animacy selection is not yet enforced.

Deferred: quantified hosts, general definite descriptions, stacked
supplements on one host, relative omission, `that` in a supplement,
PP/indirect-object/nonlocal gaps, Greek `που`, prosodic rather than written
boundaries, polar-question/answer scope, and projection through embedded attitudes or content-taking
connectives. Causal/temporal/conditional combinations with these supplements
are explicitly rejected pending a projection account, rather than placing the
supplement inside an opaque relation and claiming the intended reading.

Conjunction follows the source's initial analysis. This is not a general theory
of conventional implicature, information structure, or discourse attachment.
The native implementation does not modify the TTR grammars. Their related
templates need a separate identity/scope audit before any parity claim.

## Validation

`tests/test_nonrestrictive_relatives.py` checks exact meanings, identity of
the shared head, local witness scope, Coq exports, unresolved/unsupported
controls, actual LINK/unfixed/MERGE operations, and preservation of the
supplement across alternative complete analyses. Existing restrictive,
complement, modifier, clause, dialogue, paragraph and TTR identity tests
form the regression boundary. Negative parser outcomes retain the app's
“grammaticality not assessed” label.
