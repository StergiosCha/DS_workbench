Three further fragments illustrate how published DS analyses can be turned
into executable grammar programs. A Classical conditional beta uses situation
arguments, LINK and $\tau$ terms for examples such as \emph{If John cries,
Mary laughs}, following \citet{G11}. Native Classical and Constructive English
allow feedback during an unfinished sentence, as in A: \emph{the man},
B: \emph{mhm}, A: \emph{walks}, adapting \citet{HE21}. A separate TTR beta
lets laughter target a completed individual or situation: \emph{John sees a
telescope [laugh]} permits both targets \citep{MEMH26}. All three run through
DS actions without model assistance. These are limited fragments of the
published accounts; quotation and the fuller temporal analysis remain to be
implemented.
